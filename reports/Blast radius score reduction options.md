# Selection precision, not CPU or workers, unlocks 74 seconds

**A 74 s cut is reachable on mutants, holdout2 and holdout2u. It is borderline on holdout1 and impossible on the public sample.** In every case the seconds come from selecting tests more precisely, not from CPU, scheduling or worker count. The sample's whole gap to the oracle (only the failing tests, packed optimally) is **2.725 s**, and its container CPU is about 2 s, so its theoretical ceiling is about 4.7 s ([rows.json](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/rows.json)). The other sets have far more room: **holdout1 308.8 s, holdout2 and holdout2u 311.2 s each, mutants 738.4 s**. Most of that room sits in three slow tests landing in selections where they do not fail (`test_execute_tpcds` 21.45 s, `test_max_nodes` 28.82 s, `test_generator_memory` 6.69 s) and in "run everything" fallbacks after a failed import probe. The scheduler is already provably optimal (0.000–0.004 s recoverable), and every worker count is the optimal one for its commit. Container CPU per set is only about 2/6/6/11 s, so the CPU side can give back 1–3 s per set, never 74 s. Three re-entry points measured together reach **−74.7 s on holdout2 and holdout2u with 0 misses**, and a coverage-map fix alone gives **−98.4 s on mutants with 0 misses**. Every one of these gains, however, was found and checked on the same labelled sets, so none can be claimed until it passes a fresh-seed holdout.

How to read the evidence labels used throughout: **measured** means a full replay of main's selector (which reproduces the Docker simulated numbers exactly on all five sets) with the change applied. **Upper bound** means the figure uses label knowledge that no real rule has. **Estimate** means modelled CPU. **In-sample** means the rule's shape or threshold was chosen after looking at the same commits that judge it.

## Scheduling and worker count have nothing left to give

Main's `schedule()` does a longest-processing-time pass for every worker count from 1 to 8 and keeps the cheapest `max load + 2 s × workers`. Exact branch-and-bound over all 322 commits shows it is already optimal: **0.000 s recoverable on sample, holdout1, holdout2 and holdout2u, and 0.004 s on mutants**. That is below the board's 0.1 s rounding ([scheduling analysis](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/scheduling/)). Karmarkar-Karp and Multifit were tested as alternatives. Neither does better, and Multifit is slightly worse ([scheduling analysis](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/scheduling/)). The worst-case ratios in the literature never apply here, because most selections are 1–13 tests dominated by one long test. In 14 of 19 non-empty sample commits, and 53 of 91 on mutants, the makespan simply *is* the longest test ([scheduling analysis](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/scheduling/)).

Worker overhead is real: **42 s on sample, 178 s on holdout1, 166 s on holdout2, 160 s on holdout2u, 304 s on mutants**, or 29–43% of simulated time. But no commit uses more than 4 workers, and none would gain from a different count. The full suite packs onto 4 workers for exactly its lower bound of 36.817 s, because `test_max_nodes` (28.8 s) caps parallelism ([scheduling analysis](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/scheduling/)). The only ways to avoid the 2 s-per-worker floor are to send an empty selection or to drop the test that sets the makespan. Both are selection decisions. Scheduling also cannot cause or prevent misses, and it costs about 45 ms of CPU across all five sets.

This structure gives a decision rule that the repository does not use. Since packing is exact, a test's marginal cost Δ(t) = cost(S) − cost(S without t) can be computed directly. It is zero for nearly every test, because they fit in slack under the bottleneck. Dropping a test lowers expected cost only if P(fail) < Δ/300. That puts break-even at about **6% for tpcds, 8% for max_nodes and 1.5% for generator_memory** ([scheduling analysis](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/scheduling/)). The measured failure rate of tpcds when it is selected is **24–38%** ([bounds.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/external_methods/bounds.py)). So probabilistic or ML-style dropping, the Facebook predictive-selection model that accepts missing about 5% of failures ([Machalica et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05286)), loses money here. Precision work should go only to critical-path tests, and only through a cheap static or profile argument, not by running them.

## CPU and verifier trades are worth one to three seconds per set

Phase-level cgroup measurements in Docker with the official flags show that **probes and the verifier make up 85–90% of container CPU**. Start-up, map loading and profile loading together cost about 0.08 s clean ([CPU summary](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/cpu/summary.json)). One caveat applies to every CPU number in this section: they were taken while another labelling job kept the host load at 6–10. Absolute CPU came out 1.5–2.3× the quiet-machine figures. Clean values below are the contended values scaled by about 0.5, which is an inference, not a measurement ([CPU summary](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/cpu/summary.json)).

| Knob or change | Simulated Δ | CPU Δ (contended) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BR_VERIFY=0` | +4.1 to +11.0 s worse | −3.6 to −8.6 s | measured; verifier pays except near break-even on holdout1 |
| `BR_PROBE_SERVER=0` | 0 | +2.8 to +8.5 s worse | measured |
| `BR_VERIFY_MAX=5`, `BR_VERIFY_START=0.1` | 0 | noise | measured; same job list |
| Partial verification (verify the safe subset) | −0.3 to −4.1 s | +3.4 to +29 s | modelled; net negative |
| **Targeted probes** (import only patched modules and their reverse importers) | 0 | **−1.6 / −2.5 / −3.2 s** (holdout2 / holdout1 / mutants) | **measured in Docker, identical verdicts, 0 misses** |
| Verifier forked from an `import sqlglot` checkpoint | 0 | about −0.9 to −1.1 s clean | estimate |
| Allow `test_generator_memory` in the verifier with fail-fast | −18.6 / −18.4 s (holdout2 / mutants) | about +11 s clean | estimate; net about +7 clean, about −4 contended |

Sources: [CPU summary](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/cpu/summary.json), [targeted-probe selector](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/cpu/sel_targeted.py), [partial.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/partial.py).

The verifier buys simulated seconds at 0.5–1.4 CPU seconds each. On holdout1, 10 of its 12 runs only confirm failures, because the analysis has already pruned the harmless tests ([CPU summary](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/cpu/summary.json)). Targeted probes are the one measured CPU cut that keeps behaviour identical. They are the "reverse import closure" option that docs/next-steps-plan.md lists as not started ([next-steps plan](https://github.com/Danixjg/blast-radius/blob/08aeb05/docs/next-steps-plan.md)). The `test_generator_memory` fail-fast trade only comes out positive on a quiet machine, and its 1.5× CPU factor for that test is extrapolated. **No CPU-side combination exceeds about 3 s clean on any set**, so CPU work is a tie-breaker, not a route to 74 s.

## Seven selection re-entry points are not in the repository

docs/next-steps-plan.md Steps 1 and 2, the short-circuit part of Step 3, and Steps 4.1/4.2 are already in main ([next-steps plan](https://github.com/Danixjg/blast-radius/blob/08aeb05/docs/next-steps-plan.md)). The remaining gap splits roughly evenly across three buckets: breaking commits that over-select (148–172 s per holdout), harmless commits that still select tests (65–93 s), and full runs (68–74 s) ([rows.json](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/rows.json)). The candidates below are not in the repository. Each is listed with its measured or bounded gain.

**R1. Container-vs-`None` equality is identity (build-time profile fix).** h33727 changes `escapes is None` to `escapes == None` on a `set[str]`. The profile records `eq` as unknown because `set.__eq__` is not in `_PURE_EQ`, so `eq_swap_mask` runs all 1,620 tests for **0 failures** ([cmpchk.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/cmpchk.py)). Marking list/tuple/dict/set/frozenset-vs-None as pure makes the commit select nothing: **−36.8 s on holdout2 and holdout2u, measured by an in-memory fact edit** ([eqsim.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/eqsim.py)). This is principled rather than tuned, since those types return NotImplemented and fall back to identity. A full profile rebuild could only lower selections further, but that rebuild has not been measured.

**R2. Do not charge import-time lines in spawned pool workers to the test (coverage map).** `covplugin.py` prewarms only in the pytest parent. In ProcessPoolExecutor children, lazy imports therefore record def/class lines as test lines: 3,804 of tpcds's 9,176 recorded lines are import-time lines. Clearing those bits for the 227 executor/optimizer tests and replaying the full selector gives **holdout1 −27.3 s, mutants −98.4 s, other sets 0, 0 misses** ([mkmapB.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/external_methods/mkmapB.py)). This was simulated by editing the map, not by rebuilding it. profile.pkl may carry the same contamination, which would add gains that have not been measured.

**R3. Scope-aware name dependencies for removed imports (NameRTS-style).** h33758 removes `from functools import reduce` and selects 1,592 tests. Only line 715 reads `reduce`. Selecting that line's coverers gives 4 tests, all 3 failing tests included: **−34.0 s on holdout2 and holdout2u, measured selection on that commit** ([h33758.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/external_methods/h33758.py)). NameRTS reports skipping 69.9% of test files while staying safe on 99.6% of commits in Python projects ([arXiv 2605.25356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25356)). Importers, star-imports, `__all__` and `getattr` users must still be kept.

**R4. Fixture-entry dependencies.** tpcds reads only the SQL half of each `tpc-ds.sql` pair. h33743 and m1x000 edit only the expected-output half, yet they select tpcds anyway. Comparing the parsed (meta, sql) lists gives **−15.3 s on holdout2/2u and −15.3 s on mutants** at about 0 CPU ([dropsave.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/external_methods/dropsave.py)).

**R5. Narrow `inst_mask` for call-free class-attribute edits (in-sample).** Using readers ∩ instantiators instead of all instantiators gives **holdout1 −11.85, holdout2/2u −31.18, mutants −54.54 s, 0 misses** ([driver7.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/driver7.py)). Without the "no Call/Lambda" guard it misses test 807 on h12134 and costs 300 s ([driver6.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/driver6.py)). The guard was shaped after that miss.

**R6. Drop `test_generator_memory` when only inst/name chains select it and the selection has at most 500 tests (in-sample).** This gives **holdout1 −14.1, holdout2/2u −34.0, mutants −46.6 s, 0 misses**, but adds only 3–7 s on top of R5 ([pp2.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/pp2.py), [combo.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/combo.py)). The test fails as an xdist artifact when 61 or more tests fail, and the 500 threshold is a proxy for that.

**R7. Dynamic module-request map on probe failure (Ekstazi-style).** Today any probe failure runs everything. The ceiling is **holdout1 −68.1, holdout2 −37.5, mutants −70.4 s (upper bound)** ([breakdown.json](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/external_methods/breakdown.json)). Two static substitutes were tried and both are unsafe. The reverse-import closure missed tests on 6 of 10 commits, including 729 of 1,088 on h12162 through lazy dialect `__getattr__` ([expA2.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/external_methods/expA2.py)). A file_mask ∪ group_mask variant missed test_enum and executor tests ([driver2.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/driver2.py)). This matches Ekstazi's finding that only dynamically recorded dependencies are safe ([Gligoric et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2771783.2771784)).

A CPU-spending candidate, **C, a stage-checkpoint fingerprint for tpcds** (Elbaum's differential-unit-test idea, [Elbaum et al.](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1004&context=csearticles)), left 11 of 12 candidate patches with identical optimizer/plan fingerprints. It is worth about **38 / 21 / 28 s net** on holdout1 / holdout2 / mutants, at 3.5–6.2 s host CPU per check ([stage.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/external_methods/stage.py)). It is not safe yet: it needs stage-split coverage, and the DuckDB transpile string must be added to the fingerprint.

## Per-set verdict on the 74 s target

The R1+R5+R6 combination is the only one replayed jointly ([combo.py](../research_notes/Blast%20radius%20score%20reduction%20options/artifacts/selection-gap/combo.py)). Other sums add per-commit gains over commits that are mostly different. The "type-invariant is/== swap" idea from the external-methods notes targets the same commit as R1 (h33727), so it is not counted twice.

| Set (base) | Gap to oracle | Best route to −74 s | Expected total | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sample (97.08) | 2.7 s | none; ceiling about 4.7 s with CPU | ≥ 92.4 | **not reachable** |
| holdout1 (618.61) | 308.8 s | R2 −27.3 + R5/R6 −15.2 (partly overlapping) + C −38 net, or R7 | about 540–550 | **borderline**: measured items reach about 28–43 s; 74 s needs C (estimate) or R7 (bound) |
| holdout2 (545.40) | 311.2 s | R1+R5+R6 = **−74.7** (joint, measured); or R1 −36.8 + R3 −34.0 + R4 −15.3 ≈ −86 (additive, measured per commit) | about 459–471 | **reachable**; the first route is in-sample, the second less tuned but still found on these commits |
| holdout2u (534.17) | 311.2 s | same as holdout2 (all affected commits are shared) | about 448–459 | **reachable**, same labels |
| mutants (1065.36) | 738.4 s | R2 −98.4 alone; + R5 −54.5 | about 912–967 | **reachable** on one principled, measured change |

Targeted probes add about −0.8 to −1.6 s clean on top, measured in Docker. Only R1, R2 and R4 have a safety argument independent of the labels. R5 and R6 are explicitly in-sample, and R3 and C were each designed around specific commits. **None of these sets is a valid held-out test of the rules derived from it.**

## Ranked experiment plan

Ranking is by measured gain per unit of risk, cheapest and most principled first. Every step should go through the repository's gate: freeze the rule, then score a fresh-seed generated holdout in Docker, with paired, interleaved CPU runs on a quiet host.

1. **R1, eq-purity for container-vs-None** in `brprofile.eq_pure`. Rebuild profile.pkl and rerun build/test_profiler.py parity. Expected −36.8 s on holdout2/2u. Low risk.
2. **R2, child-process prewarm** in `covplugin._child_setup`. Rebuild map.pkl and profile.pkl and validate all five sets. Expected −27.3 on holdout1 and −98.4 on mutants, possibly more from the profile. Low risk.
3. **R4, fixture-entry comparison** for tpc-ds pairs. Expected −15.3 on holdout2/2u and mutants. Low risk.
4. **R3, scope-aware import-removal rule.** Expected −34.0 on holdout2/2u. Low to medium risk; keep importers, star-imports and getattr users.
5. **Targeted probes** with the forward-import map stored in map.pkl. −1 to −3 s CPU, identical selections.
6. **R5 + R6**, frozen as measured. Accept only if a fresh holdout shows 0 misses, because one miss (300 s) erases four sets' worth of gains.
7. **R7, record per-test module requests** (`__import__`/`import_module` hooks, pool children included), and let the probe report every failing module. Up to −68 / −37.5 / −70 s.
8. **C, tpcds stage fingerprint**, only after stage-split coverage exists and the DuckDB transpile string is in the fingerprint, gated to commits where tpcds is the bottleneck.
9. **CPU-sensitive trades** (generator_memory fail-fast in the verifier, per-dialect subtest verification, a forked verifier). Re-measure on a quiet machine first.

Drop from consideration: scheduler alternatives, worker-count changes, `BR_*` knob tuning, partial verification and probabilistic dropping. All were measured or modelled as zero or net negative.

## Conclusion

The headroom has moved. Earlier gains came from broad rules that emptied many cheap selections. What remains is a handful of commits where **one slow test, or one fallback to "run everything", sets the cost floor**. That is why rules removing hundreds of cheap tests save almost nothing, while value-level evidence about three specific tests is worth tens of seconds. The most valuable next fixes are build-time corrections to the evidence itself (the profile's equality facts and the coverage map's child-process lines), not new runtime machinery: they cost no CPU and need no thresholds tuned on the labels.

The binding constraint is now validation, not ideas. With a 300 s penalty per miss, a rule that saves 30 s per set must be right on more than nine of ten hidden commits it touches. The labelled sets have been mined to the point where they can no longer certify that. Generating and labelling a fresh-seed holdout is a prerequisite for claiming any of the 74 s, and should come before implementing R5, R6 or C.
